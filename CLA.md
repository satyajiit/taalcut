# Taalcut individual Contributor Licence Agreement

**Inactive proposal.** Taalcut uses the contribution policy in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). This
document is not a condition of contribution, collects no signatures and grants no rights while inactive.

**Draft — requires counsel approval (human task H6)**

This is a draft. It is not legal advice, and it is not in force. Counsel and the product owner must approve it before anyone is asked to sign. There is no incorporated company yet. Counsel must name the legal entity that receives these rights before this agreement is used (human task H6). Do not invent that entity, and do not treat a pull-request comment as a binding signature while this line is still a draft.

Until counsel names that entity, the grant below is written to **the Taalcut maintainers**: the people who maintain the Taalcut project. That phrase is a placeholder for the future entity. It is not a company name.

This text is original. Its shape follows two agreements that were read for this draft, and it does not copy them:

- Apache Software Foundation Individual Contributor License Agreement v2.2, <https://www.apache.org/licenses/icla.pdf>, with the purpose of those agreements at <https://www.apache.org/licenses/contributor-agreements.html> and the patent-grant FAQ at <https://www.apache.org/licenses/cla-faq.html>.
- Grafana Labs Software Grant and Contributor License Agreement (the Apache-based text, marked v r190612), <https://grafana.com/docs/grafana-cloud/developer-resources/contribute/cla/>. The entry point named in PRD section 10.3 is Grafana's contributing guide, <https://github.com/grafana/grafana/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md>, which links to a CLA page. That linked URL returned 404 when it was fetched; the Cloud documentation URL above is the agreement text that was actually read.

Grafana is the model for keeping dual licensing open. A Developer Certificate of Origin only records that you can contribute under the licence already on the file. It does not let the maintainers offer the same contribution under a second licence. That is why this agreement exists (PRD section 10.3).

## 1. Who signs, and who receives the grant

You are the person signing. "You" means that person. It does not mean your employer, unless you have your employer's written permission for this contribution and you are signing on the strength of that permission.

You grant the rights in this agreement to the Taalcut maintainers. When counsel names the legal entity (H6), that entity takes the place of this placeholder. Rights already described here are meant for that entity, but they should not be relied on until counsel has named it and approved this draft.

## 2. What counts as a contribution

A contribution is code, documentation, or other material you deliberately send for inclusion in Taalcut. A pull request, a commit, and a patch all count. Something you clearly mark in writing as "Not a contribution" does not.

You keep ownership. This is a licence, not a transfer of title. Aside from the rights you grant here, you keep every right to use your contribution for any other purpose.

## 3. Copyright grant

You grant the Taalcut maintainers a standing, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, irrevocable copyright licence in each contribution. The maintainers may copy it, adapt it, display it, perform it, publish it, distribute it, and license it onward, including through more than one level of sublicence.

The grant is wide enough that the maintainers may relicense the contribution. They may keep distributing it under AGPL-3.0-or-later, and they may also license it on other terms, including a commercial licence offered alongside AGPL-3.0-or-later. Dual licensing is the point of this agreement. A DCO alone does not give that right.

People who receive a copy from the maintainers get the rights stated in the licence on that copy. Your grant is what allows the maintainers to put a different licence on a copy, including a commercial one. It does not, by itself, take the public tree off AGPL-3.0-or-later.

## 4. Patent grant

This patent grant has the same job as section 3 of the Apache ICLA: cover the claims that your contribution actually practices, and stop the licence for someone who sues over that contribution. It is not a grant of every patent you own.

You grant the Taalcut maintainers, and anyone who receives the project from them, a standing, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free patent licence to make, have made, use, offer to sell, sell, import, and otherwise transfer the project. The licence reaches only those patent claims you are allowed to license that your contribution necessarily infringes, either on its own or combined with the project as the project stood when you submitted that contribution.

Work that other people add later does not pull extra claims of yours into the grant. A claim you become able to license later is included only if it already read on that same contribution as you submitted it. The FAQ linked above is the explanation of that boundary in the Apache text; this paragraph is the boundary this draft intends, in its own words.

If a person or company files a patent case against you or against anyone else (a cross-claim or counterclaim counts) saying that your contribution, or the project you contributed to, infringes a patent directly or by contribution, then the patent licence this agreement gave that person or company for that contribution or that project ends on the day the case is filed.

## 5. You have the right to grant this

You state that you are allowed to give the rights above.

The contribution is your own work, or you have written permission from the employer (or other person) that owns it. Written permission includes an employer's waiver of any claim that would stop you contributing. If you are unsure whether your employer owns the work, do not sign until you have that permission in writing.

If you want to send material that is not yours, do not bury it in a contribution under this agreement. Identify the source and every licence or other limit you know about, including patents and marks, and mark the material as submitted on behalf of that third party.

If you later learn that one of these statements is wrong, tell the maintainers.

## 6. No support is required

You do not have to maintain, fix, or support a contribution after you send it. You may support it if you want to, for free or for a fee, or not at all. Unless a written deal or the law says otherwise, you offer the contribution as it is, with no warranty.

## 7. No trademark grant

You grant no right in the name "Taalcut", the logo, or any other mark. The name and the logo stay under [TRADEMARKS.md](TRADEMARKS.md). This agreement does not let you use them, and it does not move them to you.

## 8. The project licence stays AGPL until the maintainers change it

The code in this repository remains under AGPL-3.0-or-later until and unless the maintainers relicense it. Signing does not change the licence on the public tree today. It only adds the relicensing right in section 3.

## 9. How you sign

Nothing collects or enforces signatures: the repository runs no signing check. Do not post a signature while the agreement is a draft.

After counsel and the product owner approve the agreement and name the legal entity, the planned signing sentence is:

`I have read the CLA Document and I hereby sign the CLA`

Under that future enabled process, the comment records a signature. A comment that says `recheck` only asks the workflow to look again. It is not a signature.

While the draft banner at the top is still in place, the agreement is not in force. Counsel approval (H6), a named legal entity, and the remaining setup in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) come before enabling signature collection or enforcement.
